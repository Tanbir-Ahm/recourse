# Act shelf: how well does the search find the right section? (2026-10-16; numbers re-run 2026-10-17)

Read this before trusting any number below. The questions and the "right" sections were chosen by Claude (an AI), not by a lawyer; the run involved no answer-writing model, so it says where the search puts a section, nothing about the answers built on it.

**Re-run on 2026-10-17.** The first run (2026-10-16) was made on text that still carried three kinds of noise: 63 sections began with their own heading twice, PC Act s.17A held 8,500 characters of Jammu and Kashmir / Ladakh text (84% of the section), and nine words were wrong (see `acts/README.md` in the research repository and `build_act_chunks.py`, check 4). All of it was repaired, the 69 changed sections were re-embedded (about 20,000 tokens, $0.002), and the same 75 frozen questions were run again. First place moved from 73% to 76%, the top 5 stayed at 95%; the same four questions miss. That is no proof that the clean text searches better (75 questions cannot show a three-point difference), only that cleaning did not hurt. The numbers below are the re-run; the 2026-10-16 numbers are in git history (`EVAL_RESULTS.json` of commit `b9d4de1`).

## What was measured

- `eval_questions.json`: 75 questions, 15 per Act (PC Act, POCSO, UAPA, PMLA, SC/ST Act), half in lay wording and half in lawyers' wording. For each, Claude wrote the section(s) it judged to answer the question (`expected`) and other sections that would be a reasonable first look (`also_ok`), from the Act's own section list and text. The file was committed (`da6817a`, hash `8fe6280a105d…`) before any retrieval number existed; `eval_act_shelf.py` refuses to run if it has changed since.
- `eval_offtopic_questions.json`: 10 questions that name a shelf Act and ask for something it does not contain (committed before any score was read), to see what "nothing relevant" scores.
- The questions are embedded with voyage-law-2 as queries (as the site does) and ranked, within the question's own Act, against the section embeddings. Billed: about 2,000 tokens (a fraction of a cent). Raw results: `EVAL_RESULTS.json` (every question, rank, top-5 sections and scores).

## Results (semantic search within the Act, 75 questions)

| | first place | top 3 | top 5 | top 8 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| the section Claude marked as the answer | 76% | 91% | 95% | 97% |
| that section or a reasonable first look (`also_ok`) | 83% | 93% | 99% | 100% |

By Act (top 5, strict): POCSO 15 of 15, PC Act 14, PMLA 14, SC/ST 14, UAPA 14. Lay wording 91%, lawyers' wording 98%. A plain keyword (BM25) ranking was worse (top 5: 80%), and fusing the two did not beat meaning alone (93%), so the shelf uses meaning alone.

**With 75 questions this can show a large problem, not rank two close methods.** The four questions whose marked section missed the top 5:

- SS02 "Is anticipatory bail available to an accused under the SC/ST Atrocities Act?" (right: s.18, which says only "Section 438 of the Code not to apply"). The statute never uses the words "anticipatory bail"; the search put it 8th of 26. This is the most litigated question about this Act, so the shelf adds a small deterministic pointer for it (see "What the numbers decided"). That pointer was added after seeing this miss, so the result for this question is not independent evidence.
- PC11 (agent who collects a bribe: marked s.12, found 7A/7/8, all reasonable), UA07 (seizure of property: marked s.25, found 24A/26, reasonable) and PM11 (which court tries the offence: marked s.43, found s.44 first, which also answers it). Counted as hits in the second row.

## What the numbers decided

1. **Search by meaning alone, ranked within the Act, top 5** (top 3 per Act when a question draws two Acts).
2. **No high score threshold.** Scores inside one Act are compressed: the marked section often scores 0.35 to 0.45 and the wrong first place 0.40 to 0.50. Keeping only sections scoring 0.42 or more would have found the marked section for 59 of 75 questions instead of 71 (at a floor of 0.30).
3. **A floor of 0.22**, under which nothing is handed over. The first draft of this note said 0.30: every one of the 75 gold questions clears it (the lowest best-score is 0.318). A second look, at eight short follow-up questions written after the gold run ("Is anticipatory bail available under the UAPA / the PMLA / in a POCSO case ...?", not frozen, not part of the numbers above), showed the right section as the closest one at only 0.236 (PMLA s.45), 0.244 (POCSO s.31) and 0.277 (UAPA s.43D) (scores of the 2026-10-16 embeddings; those eight questions were not frozen and were not run again): a floor of 0.30 would have handed over nothing for them. Of the 10 off-topic probes, 6 score 0.172 to 0.211 (nothing handed over at 0.22), 4 score 0.263 to 0.380 and pull in sections that do not bear on the question (short title, definitions, repeal and saving, and in one case the long punishments section of the SC/ST Act). The floor is a judgement call from about 18 questions, not a measured optimum; it matters only for short questions with few matching words.
4. **A cap of 14,000 characters in all** (a few sections are long: SC/ST s.3 is 11,800 characters, PMLA s.2 11,000; PC s.17A was 10,200 until its Jammu and Kashmir text was set aside, it is 1,700 now). The top 5 had a median of 8,200 characters (about 2,100 tokens) and a maximum of 24,500; about a fifth of the questions would exceed 16,000. Run on the 29 questions the Directory detects, caps of 18,000 and 14,000 handed the marked section over equally often (28 of 29), 12,000 and 10,000 less often (27): 14,000 was chosen, which keeps the largest payload near 3,600 tokens.
5. **Pointers for vocabulary gaps** (`manifest.json`, per Act): a short list of everyday phrases mapped to the sections they mean, applied only when the question contains the phrase. One entry so far (SC/ST: anticipatory bail, section 438).

## Two things that matter more than these numbers

- **The shelf is only searched when the Act Directory spots the Act in the question** (its name or one of its listed phrases). Of these 75 questions, which were written to test the search and often leave the Act unnamed, the Directory would have spotted the right Act in 29 (39%): PC Act 4 of 15, POCSO 5, UAPA 6, SC/ST 7, PMLA 7. Real questions that do not name the Act get no shelf text, exactly as today.
- **Searching the shelf for every question does not work.** Tried: the best shelf score across all 245 sections, for the 75 gold questions and for the 182 other questions in the research repository (arrest, bail, FIR and similar, which have nothing to do with these five Acts). The best of those 182 scored up to 0.47 against the shelf (a question about section 498A scored 0.47 against SC/ST s.18A); a threshold of 0.50 has no false hits among the 182 but finds only 45 of the 75 gold questions and none of the 11 real narrative questions the Directory ties to these Acts. Not built.

## After the wiring: the whole path on the same 75 questions (offline, stored question vectors, no cost)

`act_shelf.search` run as the site would run it (the Directory spots the Act first; then named sections, pointers, the closest five): the Directory found the right Act for 29 of the 75; for those 29 the marked section was handed over 28 times and a reasonable first look the 29th (PM11: s.44 instead of s.43). Counting all 75, **the marked section reached the answer model for 28 (37%)**; for the other 46 nothing from the shelf is handed over (the Act is not named), exactly as today. Sections handed over per question that draws the Act: 4.9 on average, 5 at most; characters (with the 14,000 cap): median 6,200, largest 13,800 (about 3,600 tokens). The pointer for anticipatory bail is in this run, so SS02 is no longer independent evidence.

## The two short Acts added on 2026-10-17 (Muslim Women (Protection of Rights on Marriage) Act 2019, Prevention of Damage to Public Property Act 1984)

Same caveats as above: the questions and the "right" sections are Claude's, not a lawyer's; no answer-writing model was involved; nothing here says how the answers read.

- `eval_questions_pair.json`: 24 questions (12 per Act, 12 in lay and 12 in lawyers' wording, 4 marked "hard" by Claude before the run). Written after the text was loaded and embedded and before any question was run against it; hash `7083910d2231…`; the key was not edited after a run (`corrections_log` is empty). `eval_offtopic_questions_pair.json`: 6 questions that name one of the Acts and ask for something it does not contain (hash `fe0340b98b67…`). Raw results: `EVAL_RESULTS_PAIR.json`.
- **Meaning search inside the Act** (the way the long Acts are searched): the marked section was first for 79%, in the top 3 for 92%, in the top 5 for 96% (23 of 24) and in the top 8 for 100%; counting a reasonable first look (`also_ok`), the top 5 held it for 24 of 24. Muslim Women 11 of 12 in the top 5, Public Property 12 of 12. A plain keyword ranking did no better (top 5: 92%; fused with meaning: 96%). The one miss: MW06 "My husband said talaq in 2018, before this law was passed. Can he still be punished?" (right: section 1, which carries the date the Act came into force; it came 8th of 8).
- **What an Act of seven or eight sections does to a score.** The six off-topic questions scored 0.48 to 0.60 at best against the Act they named; the 24 gold questions' lowest best-score is 0.40 and their median 0.53. In an Act this short every section scores high against every question, so the floor of 0.22 filters nothing and a score cannot say "this Act has nothing on your question".
- **Why these two Acts are handed over whole.** With the ordinary shelf path (closest five, floor, cap) the real search function (`act_shelf.search`, stored question vectors, whole-Act flag removed in a temporary copy) handed over the marked section for 22 of the 24 questions, a reasonable first look for 23. The two it missed were both among the four questions the key had flagged as hard before the run: MW06 (section 1, above) and PD12 (section 6, the Saving section, which the shelf by rule never ranks by meaning and hands over only when a question names it by number). The Acts are 3,302 and 3,813 characters in all (about 1,000 tokens each), less than the five-section payload of a long Act, so the manifest marks both `"whole_act": true` and the shelf hands over every section, in the Act's own order, with no question vector (no Voyage call). Run through the real function this gives the marked section, and the `also_ok` ones, for 24 of 24, and the code refuses the flag on any Act over 6,000 characters. **24 of 24 is a consequence of the design, not evidence for it:** the design was chosen after those two misses were seen. Its cost is that the answer model also reads sections that do not bear on the question; its limit is that rules made under the Act and case law are not in the material (the notice and the prompt say so).
- **Regression on the five long Acts.** The 75 frozen questions were run again with the seven-Act shelf: every question's rank and top-5 sections are identical to the committed run (the five Acts' chunk files and embeddings are unchanged), and the summary figures (76% / 91% / 95% / 97%) are the same.

## The Dowry Prohibition Act (1961) and the Juvenile Justice Act (2015), added 2026-10-17 (later)

Same caveats: the questions and the "right" sections are Claude's, not a lawyer's; no answer-writing model was involved; nothing here says how the answers read. These two Acts are NOT handed over whole: the Dowry Act is 11,904 characters (13 sections), the Juvenile Justice Act 153,183 (112 sections), both well over the 6,000-character whole-Act limit, so both use the ordinary meaning-search path like the five long Acts.

- `eval_questions_jjdowry.json`: 32 questions (12 Dowry, 20 JJ; half lay, half lawyers'; 5 marked "hard"), hash `f48a03ad8b3d`, written from the two Acts' own sections after the text was loaded and machine-checked and before any question was run against it. `eval_offtopic_questions_jjdowry.json`: 6 probes, hash `01772ea6f936`. Raw results: `EVAL_RESULTS_JJDOWRY.json`.
- **Meaning search inside the Act:** the marked section was first for 72%, in the top 3 for 91%, in the top 5 for 91% (strict) and 94% counting a reasonable first look (`also_ok`); top 8 97% (100% lenient). By Act (top 5, strict): Dowry 0.92, JJ 0.90. Keyword (BM25) was far worse (top 5 0.78) and fusing did not beat meaning, so the shelf keeps to meaning alone, as for the other Acts. The three strict misses: D08 "which court may take cognizance … time limit" (marked s.7, came 6th; the retrieval led with the short title and s.8/8A/8B), J09 "a child has no parents and needs care, who decides?" (marked s.27 the Child Welfare Committee, came 18th; s.30, its functions, was 5th and is an `also_ok`), and J17 "up to what age is a person treated as a child?" (marked s.2 Definitions, came 8th; the long definitions section ranks diffusely). These are the familiar kinds of miss: a forum question and two where the answer is in a long definitions/constitution-of-body section.
- **The floor does little here too.** The six off-topic probes scored 0.40 to 0.60 at best against the Act they named; the 32 gold questions' lowest best-score is 0.375. So a score cannot say "this Act has nothing on your question", and the floor of 0.22 filters almost nothing; it only guards against a completely unrelated Act. Same as the other Acts.
- **A real India Code database defect, found by the two-way comparison and corrected against the PDF:** in the Juvenile Justice Act the definition of "Child Welfare Officer" (section 2, clause 17) had its defined term `[child care institution]` overwritten in the database by a stray fragment ("has been or is being or is likely to be") copied from clause (viii) of the definition of "child in need of care and protection". The PDF reads "an officer attached to a [child care institution], for carrying out the directions". Corrected. The JJ Act's database also drops the possessive apostrophe systematically (child's→childs, children's→childrens, person's→persons, parent's→parents) and a few hyphens (sub-section→subsection, non-compliance→noncompliance, non-governmental→nongovernmental); 30 corrections against the PDF in all, each recorded in the manifest with a reason. One difference is left and recorded (`ordered_accepted`): section 61 repeats its heading once inside an amendment bracket at the start of the body (the PDF prints it once); the repeat is harmless and changes no words.
- **The Dowry Act's section 8A is in the PDF body but omitted from the PDF's printed arrangement-of-sections** (an amendment-inserted section the front index was not updated for). A new manifest field `toc_absent` lets a section be declared absent from the printed index; the loader still verifies the section's text against the PDF's running text (ordered_compare), so the guarantee that every loaded section is in the PDF is not weakened. The Dowry Act's PDF also appends the Haryana State amendments after the central text; the database (central Act) does not carry them and we do not load State amendments, so those three PDF-only tails are recorded and left out. "therefor" in section 6 is in the PDF but glued to the next word ("therefor[or"), so it is accepted as present.
- **Regression:** the 75 frozen questions on the five long Acts and the 24 pair questions on the two short Acts give identical ranks and top-5 sections to their committed runs (the seven existing Acts' chunk files and embeddings are byte-unchanged; the 125 new records were added incrementally). `test_act_shelf.py`, `test_build_act_chunks.py` and `run_tests.py` (85 of 85) pass.
